Why

Why should you listen to me? You don't have to. But for years I've been asking the same question: What do farmers really need?

Here I write down what sticks. Including the half-finished.

What I stand for – even when it gets uncomfortable

In the product

  1. Users. Users. Users. If you don't talk to users, you're guessing. And guessing is the most expensive form of development.
  2. Honest, hard decisions. Better a clear no today than a half yes until March.
  3. No falling in love with tech. AI is a tool, not a feature. We build what solves a problem – not what shone at the last conference.
  4. No buzzword bingo. If a sentence fits into any company presentation, it says nothing.
  5. Causes, not symptoms. A workaround is a bug with good marketing.

In the team

  1. Feedback both ways. Praise when it's right. Criticism when it's right. “Fine” is not feedback.
  2. Think together instead of guarding silos. Anyone who says “not my ticket” hasn't understood the product.
  3. Show half-finished ideas. Perfect is the new too late. This blog is the proof.
  4. Unlock potential. The best part of my job: when someone suddenly can do what they never thought possible – in the team and for users.

I listen more than I talk – and then I do the math.

  • Close to customers. Rather at the kitchen table on the farm than in the third alignment meeting.
  • Close to data. I love analysing numbers. Then I ask what's behind them.
  • Close to code. I build myself: plugins for Claude Code and a development process in which AI agents work alongside the team.
Make it simple, but significant.
Hence the name BLUU: Build less · Learn · Unlearn · Understand users.

The most important things aren't in any textbook. They come from the situation: at the kitchen table on the farm, on the third call from support, in the test run that goes wrong. That knowledge has no framework and no certificate. You only get it if you're close enough. And it gets lost as soon as it disappears into a concept upstairs. Work from the textbook and you build what's in the textbook. Be there and you build what's needed.

And the product owner? A mindset from yesterday. Whoever owns a product today builds with AI, measures and stands next to the customer. I call that product engineering.
