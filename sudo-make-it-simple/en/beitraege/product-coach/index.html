404: Sparring partner not found

So get one: a Claude skill for permanent coaching. Headwind when you're off. Tailwind when you're right. Installed in a few clicks.

Why

Monday, nine a.m. Sales wants the export by March. Management wants “something with AI”. The team wants to know what it's building next week. And a customer found a bug – in production, of course.

Everyone shouts “Pull!”. And you shoot – without aiming.

Tuesday gets better. Management goes straight to the developers and decides what gets built. The developers build what sales wants. You're cut out – and your customers' priorities fall off the back.

Now nobody shouts “Pull!” anymore. They shoot themselves.

Who do you ask before you decide? Stakeholders want a yes. The team wants clarity. Your AI thinks every idea is brilliant. Honest sparring for product decisions? 404 – not found.

So get one: a few clicks, and my product coach for Claude takes your next decision apart.

02Dump your situation on it. Unfiltered.

No prompt engineering. No moaning at the coffee machine. No suffering in silence. Just get it off your chest.

Claude · Chatreal session · fictional case · shortened · translated

Coach me on a situation. Management went straight to the team. They're now building the export that sales promised a key account by March. I found out in the daily. Our customer priority #1, the end-of-day close on mobile, is now on hold.

You're naming a solution, not a problem. What problem does the key account want the export to solve? Does anyone on the team know?

“On hold” is an assumption. Did anyone decide that you can only do one of the two? Or did it just happen because nobody decided?

According to your notes, management often approaches developers directly. Then whoever reaches the team first wins.

The obvious trap: you go to management and fight for “your” priority. Then it's power against power. If you lose, you're weakened. If you win, you only confirm that this is how priorities get decided.

What did you promise the five pilot farm shops for November – and does anyone in management know about that promise?

03Take the pushback.

Now it gets uncomfortable. The coach looks for the gap in your story – including the one you built yourself.

Claude · Chatreal session · shortened · translated

I promised the pilot farm shops a start in November. Management doesn't know. I never presented the customer priority with numbers.

Management didn't override your priority. From their point of view, it didn't exist. If you don't know about a November start, you can't put it on hold.

And there's a mirror. You promised the five farm shops a date that nobody knew about internally and for which capacity wasn't secured. You both made promises to the outside without aligning internally. Sales just got to the team faster.

Which number from the pilot would you open with tomorrow – and do you actually have it?

04Take the next step. By Friday.

Pushback without a plan is just a bad mood. At the end you know what to do by Friday – and what to leave alone.

Claude · Chatreal session · shortened · translated

Ouch. I don't have a number, just quotes from the pilot conversations. What do I do by Friday – concretely?

You don't have to win anything by Friday. Your goal is that management decides consciously, knowing both promises.

Monday: understand the export. Go to sales first, not to management.
By Wednesday: turn the quotes into evidence. Quotes can be counted.
Thursday or Friday: meeting with management. Ask for a decision instead of complaining – with two or three options.
Afterwards: give the five farm shops a date you can keep.

Until Friday, better leave three things alone: pulling the team back, writing an email in anger, and promising or cancelling anything with the farm shops before the decision.

At the end, it asks whether to add the lesson to your coaching log – in your words, only with your okay.

You don't need a crisis. A normal Monday will do.

Four sentences to start with:

01Situation“Coach me: sales wants …”
02Critique“Take this story apart: …”
03Skill-Building“Test me on metrics.”
04Context-Sharpening“Is this our strategy – or just a goal?”

It won't do your homework.

No Jira stories. No stakeholder emails. No slides. It coaches – and gives you honest, hard feedback on your role and your work.
